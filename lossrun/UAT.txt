Thanks for the request. Metrics can't be added from the app itself; we add them in the data pipeline's configuration, and they appear after the next nightly refresh once the change is released. To add one, please send us:

The company (ticker).
The metric: its name and how you'd calculate it, for example "Fulfillment expense / Revenue" or "Days inventory outstanding = inventory / cost of revenue × 365".
Where the number comes from, if you know. For example, a line in the income statement or balance sheet, or a figure the company only gives in its 10-K/10-Q text or earnings release, such as RevPAR or backlog.
Scope: is it only for this company, or for every company in the same industry? Peer comparison only works when the metric is added for the whole industry.
Periods: quarterly, annual, or both.
What happens next:

Already calculated but not shown (some metrics are computed in the background): we can turn it on quickly.
Can be calculated from reported financials: we add a formula. There's no extra cost, and history goes back about 5 years and 8 quarters.
Only appears in the filing text: we read it from the filings with the AI extraction step. Every value is checked against the quoted source text, so a value appears only when the filing states it clearly.
Company-specific line items (as we did for Amazon's Technology & Infrastructure and Fulfillment costs) go into a company-specific setup and show up as extra cards on that company's page only.
We'll confirm which case applies and when it will appear.

Yes. Insider trading is tracked for every company from its SEC Form 4 filings, which officers and directors must file within two business days of a trade. Today we collect about 113,000 trades across 276 companies.

What you see in the app today

Insider cluster selling appears as a risk item under Governance & Leadership → Insider Trading. It is raised when 3 or more officers or directors sell on the open market within 30 days.
It counts open-market sales only. Pre-scheduled sales under a Rule 10b5-1 trading plan are left out (about two-thirds of all sales), and so are option exercises, tax withholding, awards and gifts. This keeps it to discretionary selling.
Around events: the item is raised to Warning when a sharp stock drop, or a drop after an earnings miss, follows within 90 days. In other words, insiders sold before bad news. So far there are about 775 such items across 153 companies, and 115 of them are Warnings.
These items also feed the AI-generated Key Insights and Strategic Risk sections.
What we can add if useful

Quarterly insider metrics as cards: Insider Net Open-Market Sales (USD) and Holdings Sold by Officers (%). They are already calculated but currently hidden, along with the other market metrics, to keep the first release to the core financial scope. We can show them again.
Tighter or looser thresholds, for example 2 insiders instead of 3, or a different window, either for everyone or for a particular industry.
Sales ahead of specific events, for example insiders selling within N days before an earnings release, a restatement, a cybersecurity incident, an executive departure, or an M&A announcement. This would be a new type of alert.
Large single-insider sales, for example the CEO or CFO selling more than X% of their holdings in a quarter.
A trade-by-trade list (who, when, how many shares, price, plan or not) on the company page.
Let us know which of these matter most and which events you care about.
